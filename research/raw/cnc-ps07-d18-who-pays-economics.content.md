# Who Pays for KrishiSetu in Odisha

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

- **The strongest direct WTP evidence is Odisha-specific but supports a very small co-pay, not a normal SaaS subscription**: In a 2026 Rice Crop Manager study covering all 30 Odisha districts, **81.3% of telephone-advisory farmers and 81.8% of in-person farmers said they were willing to pay**. Among those willing, **74.6% of telephone users and 53.9% of in-person users selected Rs 10-20**. The source excerpt does not establish the billing unit or show actual payment. Treat Rs 10 and Rs 20 as experimental price points, not proven annual prices. [1] [4]

- **Voice reminders are more defensible than app-only delivery**: Personalized telephone advisories with three crop-stage reminders produced **38% full adoption**, versus **18%** after a one-time face-to-face recommendation. Even the better result was below 50%, so IVR needs repetition, convenient timing, simple Odia, and escalation to a human rather than one outbound call. [1] [4]

- **The lead payer should be the Agriculture Department through ATMA, not the individual farmer**: The 2025 ATMA rules use a **60:40 Centre-State funding ratio**, explicitly support targeted text- and voice-based advisories through mKisan, and permit at least 10% of recurring district allocation to flow through non-government agencies, with service charges capped at 10% of eligible extension-activity cost. KrishiSetu should enter a district State Extension Work Plan as an extension and disaster-readiness service. [2] [22]

- **OSDMA is an integration and resilience co-funder, not the natural agronomy owner**: Odisha's Early Warning Dissemination System already covers **1,205 villages in 22 blocks across six coastal districts**, with 122 alert-tower locations and message, voice, and siren channels. It proves that area-specific warning infrastructure exists, but the published system does not establish farm profiles, agronomic decision rules, farmer consent, or routine advisory APIs. [3] [14]

- **PMFBY creates two real insurer rails, but neither is a blanket SaaS entitlement**: Insurers must earmark **0.5% of gross written premium** for awareness, training, and capacity building. PMFBY also mandates CROPIC/mobile workflows, supports technology-based yield estimation, and uses WINDS weather data for claims, advisories, and resilience. The guidelines do **not** require insurers to purchase agronomic advisory, so KrishiSetu needs a separate insurer contract tied to loss prevention, claims evidence, or compliant IEC activity. [4] [3]

- **Insurer-sponsored free advisory has a precedent**: Bajaj Allianz's Farmitra app allowed non-customers to obtain weather, market-price, input-location, and externally supplied agricultural advisory information **free of cost**, while also supporting PMFBY enrolment and claims. This validates a sponsored B2C model, but the source provides no scale, retention, avoided-loss, or insurer ROI data. [5] [21]

- **FPOs are the best contracting and sensor-pooling layer**: Registered FPOs under the central scheme receive **Rs 18 lakh in management support over three years**, matching equity of up to **Rs 15 lakh**, and credit-guarantee support for project loans up to **Rs 2 crore**. The scheme also provides five years of handholding and support for technology use. These are organizational and financing rails, not automatic permission to spend on KrishiSetu; the purchase must appear in an approved FPO business plan. [6] [11][13]

- **State-scale sensor procurement is real, but the discovered precedent is weather monitoring rather than plot-level agronomy**: Karnataka reports **6,505 telemetric rain gauges and 935 solar/GPRS telemetric weather stations**; a current notice references supply, refurbishment, and maintenance of 1,530 rain-gauge stations. This supports pooled infrastructure procurement, but no reliable awarded contract value was found. [7] [8][23]

- **Competitor price claims should not be invented**: Fasal publicly describes soil sensors, weather stations, irrigation and fertigation automation but posts no list price; DeHaat exposes regional-language voice calls, weather, advisory, soil testing, and input/output transactions without a public subscription amount. No defensible public farmer or FPO price was found for Gramophone or Skymet either. The market pattern is therefore free/bundled/quote-based access, not a verified farmer subscription benchmark. [8] [18][16]

- **Decision**: **Prototype GO; institutional pilot GATED; overall PARTIAL**. Build the alert-to-advice workflow now, but do not launch a paid pilot until one Agriculture/ATMA, FPO, OSDMA, or insurer sponsor signs a scope and the team validates API access, agronomic safety, delivery cost, farmer action, and payer ROI.

## 4. WHAT IS MISSING

1. **Actual payment, not just stated WTP.** The Odisha RCM study gives a powerful Rs 10-20 stated band, but no transaction, renewal, default, collection cost or billing unit. A controlled payment test is still required.

2. **Cyclone/flood-specific WTP.** RCM concerns nutrient recommendations. Farmers may value a high-stakes disaster alert differently, but that cannot be inferred without a separate experiment.

3. **Competitor invoices or rate cards.** No defensible numeric price was found for Fasal, DeHaat, Gramophone or Skymet at farmer or FPO level. Quotes may vary by acreage, crop, sensor package, service level and contract volume.

4. **Odisha-approved budget head.** The report identifies ATMA, Digital Agriculture Mission, FPO support and disaster-risk funds, but no source says that a specified amount is already available to procure KrishiSetu.

5. **KALIA eligibility.** No current official rule found in this run authorizes converting KALIA/CM-KISAN beneficiary support into a vendor subscription or sensor purchase. Treating it as a procurement pool would be filler.

6. **OSDMA and IMD integration terms.** Missing items include machine-readable alert/API access, latency, uptime, permitted reuse, district geocodes, authentication, outage handling and the authority that approves transformed agronomic advice.

7. **Communications unit economics.** No verified quote was obtained for DLT registration, SMS, outbound IVR, inbound toll-free minutes, language recording, retries, human escalation or consent management.

8. **Sensor total cost of ownership.** The evidence proves public weather networks exist, but not sensor-kit price, installation, calibration, connectivity, theft, battery replacement, annual maintenance or useful life in Odisha conditions.

9. **Awarded tender values.** A Karnataka quantity precedent was found, but not a verified awarded value. Gujarat-specific agri-IoT tender claims were not sufficiently substantiated.

10. **Insurer ROI.** PMFBY and Farmitra establish mechanisms and precedent, not the number of claims avoided, loss-adjustment cost saved, adverse-selection effect, or willingness of an Odisha insurer to pay.

11. **FPO conversion economics.** Missing: number of active Odisha FPOs with positive cash flow, decision authority, seasonal budget, procurement cycle, member density, service renewal and ability to collect a Rs 10-20 co-pay.

12. **Safety and liability.** No evidence package yet defines who signs off pre-cyclone harvest, irrigation shutoff, input relocation, drainage, salvage or re-sowing recommendations, or who bears liability for a false positive or missed warning.

### Buyer-by-buyer decision

- **State Agriculture Department/ATMA - lead buyer.** The mechanism is extension funding, district planning and farmer outreach. Its scope matches agronomic advice; its trade-off is approvals through the State Extension Work Plan and cost ceilings. Recommendation: sell a measurable district service, not a generic software licence.

- **OSDMA - warning trigger and resilience partner.** Its mechanism is area-specific emergency dissemination. Its scope is disaster warnings, not crop-stage agronomy. Recommendation: integrate OSDMA alerts and escalation, but keep recommendation ownership with Agriculture/KVK/SAU experts.

- **KVKs/SAUs/ICAR - clinical-equivalent content authority.** Their role is validating rules, providing experts and handling escalations. The ATMA guidelines expressly connect them to targeted voice/text advisory and call conferencing. [22] Recommendation: obtain signed crop-disaster playbooks before field deployment.

- **FPOs and cooperatives - buyer, aggregator and collector.** They reduce customer-acquisition cost, pool hardware and add trust. Odisha cooperatives already carry a large share of short-term crop lending, while national FPO support provides management and credit capacity. [2][11] Recommendation: contract per FPO or enrolled cluster and let the organization, not KrishiSetu, manage member verification and any co-pay collection.

- **NABARD - programme and finance partner.** Use it to identify and strengthen FPOs, converge grants/credit and document technology adoption. Do not assume NABARD will pay recurring SaaS fees without a named programme or sanction.

- **Insurers - module buyer and sponsor.** The causal mechanism is reduced loss and cheaper/faster assessment. PMFBY's IEC allocation may support awareness and training, while claims and weather modules have a closer operational fit. Recommendation: price separate advisory-campaign, claims-evidence and analytics modules; do not label the whole platform an IEC expense without written approval.

- **Input and output companies - optional sponsor.** Advisory can lower acquisition cost and generate transactions, as the DeHaat bundle illustrates. [16] Recommendation: permit sponsorship only with a vendor-neutral agronomy policy, disclosed recommendations and an audit log; otherwise trust and safety suffer.

- **Individual farmer - user and small co-payer.** The evidence supports a low-friction contribution test, not the burden of funding sensors and platform overhead. Recommendation: preserve a free public-safety alert tier and charge only for optional, personalized or premium service after payment testing.

### Minimum pilot economics dashboard

Do not forecast revenue from scheme outlays. Measure this identity instead:

`Pilot contribution = contracted institutional fee + collected farmer co-pay + approved sponsor contribution - communications - field operations - sensor depreciation/O&M - agronomy QA - support`

The pilot dashboard should report: enrolled and reachable farmers; successful SMS/IVR delivery; advisory comprehension; action completion; false-alert rate; time from alert to action; crop loss relative to a matched comparison group; claims-processing time; per-activated-farmer cost; WTP conversion at Rs 0/Rs 10/Rs 20; collection cost; FPO renewal intent; and payer-specific savings.

## 8. VERDICT

### Prototype: GO

Build the prototype now because the core workflow is technically and institutionally plausible:

1. Ingest an IMD/OSDMA-style alert or a controlled test feed.
2. Resolve affected village/FPO/farm profiles.
3. Apply signed crop-stage and disaster playbooks.
4. Generate simple Odia SMS and voice guidance.
5. Retry missed calls and permit callback or expert escalation.
6. Record alert, advice, receipt, acknowledgement and action evidence.
7. Demonstrate both pre-disaster and recovery flows.

The Odisha evidence supports repeated voice delivery, while ATMA and OSDMA provide credible institutional pathways. The prototype does not need a final subscription price or production sensor network.

### Field pilot: GATED

Do not label the field pilot "GO" until all of these gates pass:

- **Payer gate:** signed letter of intent or sanction from a district Agriculture/ATMA authority, FPO/cooperative, insurer, or resilience programme.
- **Authority gate:** written ownership of agronomic rules by a KVK, SAU or Agriculture Department expert group.
- **Data gate:** documented terms for alert, WINDS, farm-profile and geospatial data, including latency and outage fallback.
- **Communications gate:** DLT/consent process, Odia voice scripts, retry policy, call timing, toll-free escalation and verified per-farmer delivery cost.
- **Commercial gate:** randomized or stepped WTP test at Rs 0, Rs 10 and Rs 20, with actual collection and renewal rather than survey intent.
- **Hardware gate:** pooled-sensor bill of materials, calibration, O&M, connectivity and replacement plan; no farmer-owned hardware assumption.
- **Evidence gate:** matched comparison measuring action and avoided loss, not only messages sent.
- **Safety gate:** audit trail, confidence threshold, human override and a prohibited-advice list.

### Overall: PARTIAL

The buyer strategy is investable enough for a prototype and payer discovery. The revenue model is not yet forecastable. The most defensible go-to-market sequence is:

1. **State/ATMA-funded alert and advisory pilot.**
2. **FPO-based enrollment and pooled sensors.**
3. **Nominal farmer co-pay experiment, not an assumed subscription.**
4. **Insurer claims/evidence module and sponsored advisory.**
5. **Commercial sensor automation only for higher-value farms after RFQ.**

| OSDMA | Disaster-resilience project and emergency infrastructure | Cyclone/flood triggers and public warning | Strong warning capability, weak agronomy ownership | Deployed Odisha system | Near-term integration/co-funder |
| FPO/cooperative | Organization contract, approved management/project budget and member collection | Enrollment, pooled sensors, local support | Lower CAC but uneven capacity and cash flow | Official scheme plus Odisha credit-channel evidence | Near- to medium-term B2B2C |
| Insurer/PMFBY | IEC allocation, claims-technology contract or commercial loss-prevention budget | Weather, evidence, claims and sponsored advice | Incentives align with loss reduction, but advisory purchase is not mandatory | Official rules plus Farmitra precedent | Medium-term module buyer |
| NABARD/AIF | Credit, convergence, FPO promotion and eligible infrastructure | Hardware and organization capacity | Finance must be repaid or specifically sanctioned | Official eligibility and scheme evidence | Medium-term scale finance |
| KVK/SAU/ICAR | Public extension and expert participation | Rule validation and human escalation | Essential authority but not a proven software budget owner | Current ATMA rules | Immediate design partner |
| Input/output company | Sponsorship, commerce margin or acquisition budget | Bundled advice and transactions | Fast distribution but potential bias | DeHaat-style bundle, no public price economics | Optional secondary channel |
| Individual farmer | Small optional co-pay | Personalization and premium convenience | Direct WTP is low and stated rather than paid | Strong local survey, weak transaction evidence | Test only after institutional funding |

The non-obvious conclusion is that **the same feature has different buyers for different reasons**. OSDMA values reach and speed; Agriculture values correct action; an FPO values member service and pooled infrastructure; an insurer values evidence and reduced loss; the farmer values trusted, timely convenience. One undifferentiated "subscription" cannot capture all five mechanisms.

The second tension is between national fiscal scale and local addressability. The Digital Agriculture Mission, disaster-risk funds, PMFBY premium flows and FPO scheme are large, but each has restrictions and governance. The winning proposal will not say "Rs 2,817 crore is our TAM." It will name a district work plan, eligible activity, contracting authority, beneficiary cohort, delivery metric and sanction path.

The third tension is accessibility versus instrumentation. Odisha evidence favors repeated voice contact, while Fasal-like systems emphasize farm hardware and mobile control. For KrishiSetu, this implies an **edge-assisted, institution-owned sensor layer behind a voice-first user experience**. Farmers should not need to purchase or operate the sensor network to receive life- and crop-protecting guidance.
