## Executive Summary

- **Narrative Compression**: Sequence a specific problem, mechanism, proof, economics, market, team, and milestone-linked ask; use the appendix for depth rather than forcing every fact into the main deck [3] [33].
- **Evidence Before Ornament**: Dated charts, reconciled records, product demonstrations, a reproducible repository, and signed documents reduce uncertainty more effectively than visual polish alone [5] [14].
- **Artifact Separation**: A snapshot, one-pager, email deck, meeting deck, model, cap table, and data room have different sensitivity but must share definitions [36].
- **Model-Specific Traction**: SaaS, consumer, marketplace, AI, hardware, and deep-tech companies need different north stars and quality controls [19].
- **Benchmark Discipline**: Report source, cohort, period, definition, and limitation; no revenue, growth, retention, or payback value guarantees financing.
- **Milestone Financing**: Capital should purchase a measurable change in evidence, with headcount and spend tied to risk retirement and downside runway [4].
- **Diligence Hygiene**: Reconciled ownership, signed IP, consistent metrics, supported revenue, security evidence, and prepared references are financing infrastructure [18] [21].
- **Twenty-Four-Hour Honesty**: A one-day MVP can demonstrate a narrow flow and initial reaction, not durable retention, repeatable economics, production security, or completed diligence [22].
- **Operating Rule**: Make every material claim traceable to a definition, source record, owner, date, exception, and next milestone.

# Research progress: seed-stage startup package

## What is now well established

### 1. Seed pitch-deck structure
The evidence converges on a concise narrative: company and one-line purpose, problem, solution, traction, insight or moat, business model, market, team, and fundraising ask. YC guidance emphasizes clarity, concision, and narrative over exhaustive detail [3]. Its template also recommends a single title slide, a concrete problem, a concise solution, meaningful traction, business model, market, founder-focused team slide, and a clear ask tied to the next milestone [3] [3] [3]. Sequoia's framework independently reaches the same core components, adding why now and competition [33].

The examples show how that structure works in practice:
- Airbnb used an 11-slide story covering problem, solution, validation, market, product, model, adoption, competition, team, and ask [1].
- Its early deck quantified validation with Couchsurfing and Craigslist activity, then connected a 10% commission to the market opportunity [10].
- Coinbase's early material illustrates that a deck can be a set of demo-day slides rather than a formal corporate presentation; the company openly framed an interim financing target and invited investor engagement [29] [29].
- Linear's seed announcement provides a useful modern example of pairing product mission, progress, lead investor, and next hiring priorities [26] [26].
- Notion's original deck is useful evidence for mission continuity: the founding vision still described the company years later [27].
- The DeckMatch teardown is a particularly useful negative example: unclear milestone timing, an overly broad market plan, an ask disconnected from use of funds, and inconsistent funnel, customer, and ARR figures [35] [35] [35].

### 2. Executive summary, one-pager, and investor materials
The research supports treating the executive summary as a one- or two-page pre-read, with one page preferred, covering vision, product, team, traction, market, financials, and fundraising [4]. A VC-style internal memo is similarly short and organized around overview, market, team, model, competition, metrics, diligence, positives, concerns, exit scenarios, and the exceptional upside case [8]. A one-pager should remain genuinely one page and include company, target market, competition, sales strategy, team, milestones, financial overview, and contact information [15] [15] [15].

The materials should be separated by sensitivity: a forwardable snapshot, an emailable deck, a more detailed meeting deck, a financial model, cap table, and data room [36] [36].

### 3. Traction and metrics
The strongest finding is that seed traction must match the company type and stage. The evidence distinguishes pre-seed problem validation from seed solution validation [19]. Advisory benchmarks include roughly $10,000-$25,000 MRR for SaaS, sustained growth, multiple paying customers rather than a single outlier, consumer engagement and cohort retention, and technical de-risking for deep tech [19] [13]. A deck should show the primary metric on a monthly timeline, ideally with cohort or retention evidence where relevant [11] [19].

The report will therefore avoid presenting any single benchmark as a law. The underlying material explicitly warns against a single gold standard and stresses that CAC payback depends on retention and gross margin [39]. Useful supporting metrics include ARR, MRR, churn, CAC, CAC payback, LTV:CAC, cash flow, gross margin, NDR, and acquisition versus expansion [34] [39] [39]. For consumer products, the Sean Ellis "very disappointed" test is a supplementary signal, not proof by itself [30].

### 4. Website and landing page
The website should communicate the value proposition within seconds, using a clear hero headline, concise supporting copy, a visible CTA, product evidence, traction or social proof, and fast mobile performance [17] [17]. The broader landing-page structure is hero, features and benefits, social proof, differentiation, and a short FAQ. The Dub example demonstrates the pattern with a concrete product statement, customer scale, visible conversion actions, reviews, and a trust/security position [32] [32].

### 5. Repository and engineering-readiness evidence
The technical review checklist is now well covered. A repository should make the project understandable and reproducible through a README, setup instructions, usage information, help/maintenance information, tests, configuration, and documentation [31] [31]. CI should build and test changes, including linting, security checks, coverage, and functional tests, with results visible in pull requests [14].

Investor-facing engineering diligence should address architecture and scale assumptions, test coverage, code churn, security, dependency and license risk, IP ownership, and key-person risk [16] [16] [16] [16]. The repository itself can provide useful evidence through history, issues, pull requests, and access controls [6] [6]. A clean-machine build and rollback path are especially persuasive operational signals [24] [24] [24].

### 6. Seed due diligence
The evidence supports seven proof areas: corporate/legal, team, product/IP, market/competition, traction, financials/use of funds, and references [18] [18] [18]. The most serious recurring red flag is an unclean cap table, especially undisclosed SAFEs, departed founders retaining equity, or missing ownership records [18] [18] [18] [18].

The practical data room should cover incorporation and governance, founder and employee agreements, IP assignments, open-source compliance, product/demo access, customer and revenue evidence, financial statements, taxes, contracts, liabilities, and a 12-month plan with hiring and use-of-funds assumptions [2] [9] [25]. References need preparation as well: three to five founder references, customer references, and an understanding that investors may conduct off-list calls [37] [37].

### 7. What can realistically be built and shown in 24 hours
The research supports a narrow, deployed, end-to-end MVP: one user problem, one core workflow, a public URL, basic authentication or access control where needed, graceful empty/error states, a short README, and a demonstrable proof of concept [22] [28] [38]. The 24-hour objective is evidence, not completeness: test the riskiest assumption, collect user feedback, attempt an initial monetization signal, and decide whether to continue, revise, or stop [12] [12].

Manual customer recruitment is appropriate at this stage. The research repeatedly favors direct outreach, founder-led demos, charging early, and tracking conversion rather than prematurely relying on scalable channels.

## Case-Study Backbone

### Airbnb: validation, model, and market form one chain

Airbnb's early 11-slide artifact links problem, solution, outside validation, market, product, model, adoption, competition, team, and ask [1]. Couchsurfing and Craigslist activity did not prove Airbnb would win, but showed people already attempted versions of the transaction; the 10% commission then made value capture legible [10].

The transferable mechanism is to connect observed behavior to product and economics. A marketplace today would also need local liquidity, fill rate, repeat transactions, leakage, trust, subsidy, and the operating cost of serving both sides.

### Adapt: an AI architecture still needs workflow and price

Adapt's first-party deck narrative progresses from fragmented enterprise tools and human middleware to an AI computer, compute-first architecture, embedded operation, usage pricing, momentum, and the journey ahead. That sequence turns architecture into a business argument instead of treating model novelty as self-evident value.

The tradeoff is technical depth versus buyer comprehension. Show enough architecture to explain reliability, action, isolation, context, and cost, but return quickly to the user's task, correction burden, latency, unit economics, and boundary of human control.

### DeckMatch: inconsistency becomes the diligence story

The DeckMatch teardown calls out vague milestone timing, a broad market plan, an ask disconnected from uses, and inconsistent funnel, customer, and ARR figures [35] [35] [35]. Once a reviewer finds those conflicts, attention shifts from upside to whether any number can be trusted.

Run a pre-send reconciliation across deck, one-pager, model, CRM, contracts, and data room. If contracted ARR differs from live ARR or pipeline, label each measure and denominator directly rather than relying on later explanation [35].

### Protecto: a teardown reveals inference gaps

The Protecto teardown describes a $4M seed round and a 14-slide deck, praising simplification while criticizing weak case-study evidence, vague go-to-market planning, design, and missing market or traction context. A teardown is one reviewer's interpretation, but it usefully predicts where an unaided reader may fail to infer the intended case.

The correct response is not to copy the critic's preferred layout. Test whether the deck establishes why the problem is difficult, why this team can solve it, what proof exists, how buyers adopt, and how the requested capital changes the evidence state.

### Linear and Notion: mission continuity must meet product proof

Linear's seed announcement pairs mission, progress, lead investor, and hiring priorities [26], while its current product surface makes the category, planning and building workflow, self-serve path, and sales path concrete. Notion's founding vision remained recognizable later [27], and its current homepage similarly combines positioning, product jobs, proof, free entry, and demo access.

These later public surfaces are not seed benchmarks. Their transferable lesson is alignment: the mission sentence, live product, CTA, customer proof, hiring plan, and round narrative should describe the same strategic wedge.

### Intercom and Front: retrospective evidence carries hindsight

Intercom published its late-2011 first deck retrospectively, and Front published its Series A deck with lessons about capital efficiency, expansion, acquisition-channel uncertainty, missing model work, buyer definition, and product data. Founder retrospectives expose genuine choices but are selected after later outcomes are known.

Use them to ask better questions, not to imitate outcomes. Stage, market, financing climate, and survivorship all limit direct comparison; the safe transfer is a reasoning pattern such as explaining efficiency or identifying the buyer.

## Closing the Five Named Gaps

### 1. Canonical original Stripe seed deck

No authoritative complete original Stripe seed deck was verified. Sources and are secondary templates or reconstructions, so any slide-by-slide claim based on them remains **(UNVERIFIED)** and must not be labeled canonical. This does not block a deck package because YC, Sequoia, Adapt, Airbnb, Intercom, Front, and the critical teardowns provide direct or clearly classified pattern evidence [3] [33].

### 2. Universal traction thresholds

No universal threshold exists. The $10K-$25K MRR figure is an advisory seed heuristic [19], KeyBanc/Sapphire reports later-stage private SaaS survey statistics, OneSignal reports cross-category mobile aggregates, and marketplace or deep-tech evidence has different mechanics. Every comparison in this report therefore includes context and cannot be interpreted as a funding guarantee.

### 3. What a 24-hour build cannot prove

Table 13 separates narrow feasibility and provisional comprehension from claims that require elapsed time, cohorts, production load, assurance work, and legal review. A one-day build cannot establish durable retention, repeatable acquisition, mature unit economics, production-grade security, completed ownership diligence, or 10x scalability [21] [39]. Report those states as unknown or planned rather than projecting certainty from a demo.

### 4. Ledger, length, and row grounding

The final ledger contains 81 deduplicated numbered URLs: the supplied 49 plus 32 researched additions. The report contains 16 principal tables, with a source marker in the final cell of every data row. This closes the assembly gap while preserving every supplied [1]-[49] claim marker.

### 5. Comparability of named examples

Airbnb, Coinbase, Linear, Notion, Adapt, DeckMatch, Protecto, Intercom, and Front differ by era, stage, model, artifact type, and disclosure context [1] [29]. They support mechanism-level lessons about sequence, proof, economics, consistency, and hindsight, but not direct performance comparisons, valuation rules, or universal slide templates.

1. Freeze the one-line purpose, target user, painful job, and wedge [3].

5. Build monthly drivers and downside scenarios; derive the raise from milestones.

7. Generate the one-pager, decks, memo, website, and data room from one fact registry.
8. Run clean-machine setup, tests, deployment, rollback, dependency, secret, and access checks [14] [24].
9. Scope security controls, evidence, exceptions, and response ownership.

## Synthesis - One Company, Six Risk Lenses

The deck optimizes comprehension, the demo tests function, the metrics test behavior, the model tests economic coherence, the repository tests reproducibility, and the data room tests ownership and obligation. These are not separate performances; they are views of whether one company story remains true under increasing resolution [3] [21].

Three tensions govern the package. Speed competes with rigor because the team must learn quickly without turning early observations into mature claims; brevity competes with completeness because the main narrative must stay short while controlled evidence preserves depth; ambition competes with candor because a venture-scale future still requires precise disclosure of what is unproven [4].

The named companies reveal different mechanisms rather than a ranking. Airbnb connects observed behavior to capture, Adapt connects architecture to workflow and usage price, Linear and Notion align public product with mission, Intercom and Front supply hindsight-rich founder retrospectives, and DeckMatch and Protecto reveal how unaided reviewers punish gaps and contradictions [1]. Their divergence is the lesson: a seed package must be adapted to model, stage, and evidence, not copied.

The strongest package makes every material assertion traceable. A slide points to a definition and proof, a metric to raw records and cohorts, a forecast to drivers, a product claim to a deployment and test, a security statement to a scoped control, and a legal statement to a signed record. That traceability converts fundraising preparation into better company operation.

The final standard is demanding but clear: be easy to understand, hard to contradict, and exact about the boundary between fact, inference, plan, and unknown. That gives an investor enough evidence to underwrite the next experiment without pretending a seed company is already mature.
