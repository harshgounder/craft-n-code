# Crop Evacuation Physics for Odisha Flood Advisories

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

- **The crop itself is movable only in bounded forms:** harvested produce, seed, portable nursery mats, pots, and purpose-built modular systems can be relocated. A rooted field crop generally cannot be evacuated safely in 24-72 hours; the practical choices are early harvest, in-place protection, drainage, or post-flood recovery. IRRI's modified mat nursery is genuinely portable at 15-20 days, but a full-hectare nursery uses 100 m2 of mats and 4 m3 of media, so even seedling evacuation is a logistics task rather than a one-line advisory [35].

- **The 5.76% paddy number is real but often misused:** an August 2025 meta-analysis of 32 studies and 977 data pairs found a mean **5.76% yield reduction** for premature harvest relative to 35 days after heading; the reference moisture was 22%-24%. It is not an Odisha dough-stage threshold, and responses varied by zone and grain type [7].

- **A defensible paddy rule starts at maturity, not at a generic warning:** IRRI recommends harvest around 20%-25% grain moisture and when 80%-85% of grains are straw-colored; OUAT's Cyclone Dana advisory told Odisha farmers to harvest early varieties at 85% maturity and store or cover the crop safely [8][25]. Before that point, emergency harvest should require a positive expected-loss calculation, not automatic advice.

- **Lead time is mostly a labor constraint:** a Sri Lankan field study measured 233, 113.5, and 9 man-hours/ha for manual, reaper, and combine systems, while reaper and combine field capacities were 0.185 and 0.340 ha/hour [30]. With four adults working eight hours/day, the corresponding illustrative manual capacity is only **0.14 ha in 24 hours** and **0.41 ha in 72 hours**. These are planning priors, not Odisha coefficients.

- **Official Odisha guidance supports several low-regret physical actions:** pick mature pulse pods, drain paddy and vegetable fields, keep buffer or elevated nurseries, use 1.0 m broad beds with 30 cm by 15 cm furrows, mechanically support horticultural plants, and prop sugarcane. The same plan gives post-flood branching at less than versus more than 50% paddy damage [25][2].

- **Swarna-Sub1 is a strong post-flood and ex-ante variety case, not a rescue treatment:** a randomized experiment in 128 Orissa villages found about **45% higher yield than Swarna after 10 days of submergence**, with the advantage reaching about 718 kg/ha, or 66%, near 13 days; the no-flood difference was not statistically significant [29]. The action is to plant the variety before the season, then diagnose survival after drainage.

- **Seeds are easier to evacuate than crops and have demonstrated recovery value:** Odisha community seed banks dry, clean, store, and share seed; one documented system uses clay pots, straw layers, and cow-dung sealing. Navdanya reports distributing 100 quintals of 20 flood- and salt-tolerant rice varieties to 400 farmers after Cyclone Phailin [28][27]. Seed inventory and dry raised storage should therefore rank above speculative mature-plant movement.

- **Floating gardens are real but are not emergency rafts:** Bangladesh's Dhap system layers water hyacinth and bamboo, waits 8-10 days before sowing or transplanting, and supports multiple vegetable cycles on seasonally inundated land. The source does not say that completed beds are routinely relocated before a cyclone [26]. This is a pre-season production architecture, not a 24-hour evacuation feature.

- **The defensible verdict is PARTIAL:** deploy a stage-aware, labor-aware advisory engine for mature harvest, drainage, portable nursery and seed relocation, livestock evacuation, and post-flood branching. Gate or suppress mature field-plant relocation, coconut-frond tying, banana pseudostem cutting, paddy cut-and-carry, emergency trellis dismantling, farmer-built sandbag levees, and claims about Odisha "daru/machan" until locally tested.

It is not sufficient for a universal autonomous instruction to harvest an immature crop, cut a tree, construct a levee, or move a mature field plant.

## 4. WHAT IS MISSING

The exact research gap is a **local action-response surface**:

`Outcome = f(crop, variety, stage, maturity/moisture, flood depth, submergence duration, salinity, wind, forecast probability, lead time, action, labor, machine access, drying/storage capacity)`

Existing sources cover fragments. The rice meta-analysis supplies an average premature-harvest penalty, IRRI supplies maturity indicators, Odisha supplies action lists, the Sri Lanka study supplies labor coefficients, and the Swarna-Sub1 RCT supplies a submergence response. No retrieved source joins those variables into a farmer-level decision threshold.

| Missing variable | Exact study needed in Odisha | Engine output it would unlock |
|---|---|---|
| Paddy dough-to-maturity salvage curve | Crop-cut plots harvested at defined days after heading and moisture levels, followed by simulated rain/submergence, drying and milling | Variety- and stage-specific early-harvest loss function rather than a blanket 5.76% |
| Vegetables, banana and pulses | Trials comparing leave, pick/harvest, prop and drain actions across maturity classes and forecast lead times | Crop-specific marketable-yield and quality thresholds |
| Bracing and wind protection | Instrumented field trials for banana, papaya, sugarcane, mango and cashew across wind classes, pole designs and soil saturation | Expected avoided lodging/uprooting per rupee and labor-hour |
| Portable nursery physics | Time-motion study measuring wet mat mass, segment breakage, workers, trip distance, vehicle payload and establishment after relocation | Maximum movable nursery area within 24, 48 and 72 hours |
| Mature-plant movement | Small controlled trials only where agronomically plausible, with root-ball mass, mortality and yield recovery | Likely confirmation that most field crops should remain excluded |
| Local harvest logistics | Odisha district measurements of manual labor, reaper/combine availability, travel time, wet-field access, emergency wages and drying queues | Real completion time and cost, not Sri Lankan transfer values |
| Raised platforms and local practices | Ethnographic and engineering verification of named machan/daru practices, dimensions, load capacity, materials and cyclone performance | Location-specific raised-storage rule, if the practice is real and safe |
| Temporary barriers | Hydrologic design and safety review covering inflow, rainfall trapped behind barriers, overtopping and impacts on neighboring land | Authority-approved barrier advice; otherwise continued suppression |
| Post-flood recovery | Stage-by-duration trials for tiller survival, gap filling, ratooning, nutrient timing, re-sowing and salinity | Rescue-versus-replant decision by crop and remaining season |
| Low-literacy interaction | Odia and dialect IVR trials using teach-back, response time and error rates during mock warnings | Proven question sequence and message length rather than an assumed accessible interface |

Odisha's current damage procedure identifies GPS-referenced grids expected to have at least 33% crop loss and cross-checks inundation with remote sensing [68]. That is valuable labeled data, but the crop-cutting experiment occurs when paddy and other major crops are ready for harvest [68]. The research need is to connect those post-event labels to pre-event stage, action, labor and forecast data. Otherwise the platform will repeat official advice without learning which action actually paid.

## 5. HOW IT FEEDS THE ADVISORY ENGINE

### A. Low-literacy farmer-problem capture

The interface should ask one short question at a time by IVR, with keypad or spoken answers and SMS confirmation:

1. **Where is the field?** Village/panchayat plus a landmark; GPS is optional.
2. **What is at risk?** Paddy, pulse, vegetable, banana, orchard, seed/nursery, livestock or machinery.
3. **How much?** Acres or a local-area choice read aloud.
4. **What stage?** Use observable choices: "panicle just emerged," "grain soft," "most grain yellow," "mature pods visible," or "marketable fruit present," not technical stage names alone.
5. **What can the household mobilize?** Adults, hired workers, reaper/combine, vehicle, bamboo poles, tarpaulin, dry store and high ground.
6. **What is already wet or lodged?** This switches from prevention to rescue.
7. **Teach-back:** "Press 1 if you can finish before 5 PM and still evacuate safely; press 2 if not."

Odisha already has a multi-hazard warning foundation: SATARK was developed by OSDMA with RIMES and covers cyclones, floods and drought among other hazards [69]. The uniqueness is not another alert channel. It is converting a warning into a physically completable, crop-specific action after capturing missing farm state from a low-literacy user.

### B. Rule and optimization layer

| Rule/algorithm | Inputs | Decision produced |
|---|---|---|
| Maturity gate | Crop, variety, observable stage, grain moisture if available | Paddy at 80%-85% straw color or an official 85%-maturity trigger enters "harvest eligible"; immature paddy enters expected-loss evaluation [8][25] |
| Early-harvest break-even | Forecast probability `q`, predicted no-action loss `L0`, post-action residual loss `L1`, early penalty `Pe`, quality and operation cost `C` | Harvest only if `q x (L0 - L1) > Pe + C/crop_value`; initialize `Pe = 0.0576` only as a global rice prior [7] |
| Completion-time gate | Area, person-hours/ha, machine field capacity, travel, threshing, bagging, drying/covering, road cutoff, safety buffer | Recommend only the area that can be cut, secured and moved before the evacuation deadline |
| Salvage-value ranking | Expected saved crop value, food/seed criticality, completion time, reversibility and confidence grade | Rank jobs by expected saved value per labor-hour, after human and livestock safety |
| Portable-asset solver | Number of mats/pots/bags, wet unit mass, payload, load/unload time, route and trips | "Move 12 seed bags first" or "only the nursery nearest the road fits" rather than "move seedlings" |
| Crop-protection rule | Crop, wind/flood hazard, supports/materials, stage | Generate only verified actions such as drainage, pulse-pod picking, sugarcane propping and horticulture support |
| Confidence gate | Evidence grade, district validation status | A/B rules can be issued; C rules require a qualified caveat or extension-officer confirmation; D rules are suppressed |
| Post-flood classifier | Stage, variety, days submerged, salinity, surviving stand, remaining season | Drain and inspect; gap-fill below 50% damage, re-transplant above 50%, or shift to short-duration/pre-rabi crops [2] |
| Learning loop | Forecast, recommendation, user completion, GPS/inundation, crop-cut result and final yield | Re-estimate local action effects instead of treating official text as timeless truth |

### C. Ordered action output

The output should be no more than three farmer actions plus one safety stop:

> "Cyclone rain may reach your village in 36 hours. Your paddy is 85% yellow. Four workers can manually secure only about 0.18 ha before the safety cutoff. First harvest the lowest field. Second thresh or cover it and move seed bags to the raised store. Third move cattle on the mapped route with 3-5 days of fodder. Stop field work at the evacuation time."

The livestock module should pre-map primary and alternate routes, identify animals, maintain suitable transport and reserve 3-5 days of feed or fodder [66]. Machinery, pesticide, fuel and documents should move only after people and livestock are secure, with hazardous materials kept out of floodwater. The engine must never let a crop-salvage score override an official evacuation order.

## 8. VERDICT: PARTIAL

**GO now** for a minimum viable advisory engine containing:

1. IMD/OSDMA hazard ingestion and district/village targeting.
2. IVR capture of crop, stage, area, labor, machinery, storage and livestock.
3. Verified maturity-gated harvest, mature-pod picking, drainage, nursery/seed relocation, propping and safe-storage rules.
4. A labor and completion-time calculator.
5. Human/livestock safety priority, mapped evacuation and 3-5-day fodder planning.
6. Post-flood drainage, diagnosis, gap-fill/replant and short-duration crop branching.
7. Evidence grades on every generated recommendation.
8. Outcome capture linked to Odisha's GPS and crop-cutting assessment process, whose final lists cover farmers with more than 33% crop loss [68].

**Gate before autonomous release** any recommendation to harvest below validated maturity, transplant mature rooted crops, climb coconut trees, cut banana pseudostems, build field levees, conduct late orchard pruning, or rely on a named traditional practice that has not been verified locally.

**Synthesis:** the strongest solution is not "crop evacuation" in the literal sense. It is **selective conversion of an immovable standing crop into movable value**: harvest mature grain or pods, lift nursery mats, preserve seed, move harvested produce, and relocate livestock and small assets. Where conversion is premature or impossible, the engine switches to in-place protection or post-flood recovery. The decisive variables are maturity, expected avoided loss, person-hours, machine arrival, storage/drying capacity, safe cutoff time and evidence confidence. That combination is meaningfully different from a generic weather-alert application and directly addresses the official problem statement without promising physics that farmers cannot execute.
