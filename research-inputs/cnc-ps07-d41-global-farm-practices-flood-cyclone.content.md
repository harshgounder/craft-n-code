# Global Flood Farming Wisdom for Coastal Odisha

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

- **Fit The Practice To The Water Regime**: The strongest global lesson is not to "fight every flood." Mekong wetland models, Bangladesh floating beds, deepwater or floating rice, and flood-recession cultivation work only where water depth, duration, velocity, seasonality, and salinity match the practice. IUCN explicitly based its Upper Mekong models on farmer knowledge in An Giang, Dong Thap, and Long An [6]. -> The engine must classify the flood before recommending a crop response.

- **Use Low-Cost Asset Protection First**: Elevating dry seed, feed, tools, pumps, records, and small livestock is more transferable to coastal Odisha than constructing polders, chinampas, or tank cascades. Common jute-sack, mud-house, and earthen-pot seed storage can still fail in extreme floods [17]. -> Recommend sealed containers on verified high shelves or community platforms, with a confirmation step.

- **Treat Landscape Engineering As Infrastructure, Not An Alert**: Ifugao terraces and woodlots, Huzhou dyke-ponds, Sri Lankan tank cascades, and Dutch delta works change how water moves across whole landscapes. The Ifugao muyung traps precipitation and nutrients and releases them slowly to terraces below [12]. -> Put these ideas into village investment and watershed-planning modules, not a 24-hour SMS.

- **Pair Technology With Institutions**: Bali's Subak coordinates irrigation through water temples and farmer groups, while Bangladesh's Cyclone Preparedness Programme converts official meteorological warnings into sheltering, rescue, medical attention, and recovery [9][1]. -> Every alert must name the responsible human actor, shelter, route, and escalation contact.

- **Make Early Warning End-To-End**: WMO's people-centered multi-hazard model links risk knowledge, detection and forecasting, warning communication, and preparedness; only about half of countries report adequate systems, and observation gaps remain [2]. -> Sensor accuracy alone is not success; delivery, comprehension, acknowledgment, and action completion are required outputs.

- **Use Biological Tolerance Within Its Limits**: Flood-tolerant rice can reduce submergence risk, but it does not solve long-duration stagnant water, salinity, high-velocity flow, lodging, or seedling washout. Swarna-Sub1 is documented as tolerating roughly two to three weeks of submergence [23]. -> Variety advice must include crop stage, flood type, expected duration, and local release approval.

- **Borrow Bangladesh's Floating Gardens Selectively**: Baira or dhap beds use water hyacinth and other plant residues to carry vegetables and seedlings over seasonal waterlogging; local farmers have used them for about two centuries in selected southern districts [3]. -> Pilot them for homestead nutrition and nurseries in calm freshwater pockets, not as a substitute for coastal paddy or in saline, fast-moving cyclone water.

- **Keep Livestock In A Separate Workflow**: Crop messages do not cover animal identification, evacuation routes, elevated shelter, fodder, clean water, carcass handling, or veterinary follow-up. FAO's flood-emergency guidance is explicitly intended to support local-government planning for livestock management and evacuation [22]. -> Store species, headcount, mobility, shelter capacity, and fodder-days as first-class farm-profile fields.

- **Reject Attractive Category Errors**: Thai floating markets are commerce, Arkansas "flooded rice" is normally an irrigation regime, Gezira is a large gravity-irrigation scheme, and Australian floodplain harvesting is a regulated water take. Arkansas breeders historically selected rice for season-long conventional flood irrigation, not uncontrolled disaster flooding [25]. -> None should enter the advisory library as a cyclone survival practice.

- **Reuse Odisha's Existing Last-Mile Channel**: Ama Krushi, now described by its operator as the Krushi Samruddhi Helpline, already combines a short code, IVR, live-agent access, tailored weekly calls, and stored advisory history [30]. -> Add evidence-graded disaster rules to this channel rather than requiring a smartphone-first replacement.

## 4. WHAT IS MISSING

### 4.1 Hazard specificity

"Flood" is not one state. The engine still needs Odisha-calibrated thresholds for pluvial waterlogging, river overflow, embankment breach, tidal backwater, saline storm surge, flash flow, and long-duration stagnant water. A floating garden that works in calm monsoon water may become debris in a cyclone current; a submergence-tolerant rice can still fail under salinity or prolonged stagnation.

### 4.2 Current-use and outcome evidence

Many heritage descriptions explain mechanisms but not comparative yield, avoided loss, labor, cost, maintenance failure, or adoption rate. The inventory therefore separates **documented existence** from **proven disaster performance**. Particularly weak entries are Myanmar's delta package, Pakistan's *kacha/khaddar* melon claim, Ethiopia's Tekeze claim, Madagascar's *filovana*, and Indonesia's combined raft/sago claim.

### 4.3 Seed and livestock detail

The global list is crop- and water-engineering-heavy. It lacks sufficiently localized specifications for waterproof seed packaging, safe storage height, duplicate community seed lots, fodder reserves, animal loading order, shelter stocking density, disease surveillance, carcass disposal, and post-flood veterinary triage. The evidence that common seed containers can fail in extreme floods shows why "store seed safely" is too vague [17].

### 4.4 Social feasibility

Subak, tank cascades, CPP, Dutch water planning, and Ifugao terraces all depend on institutions, labor, maintenance, and legitimate allocation rules. A physical copy without governance can fail. The system needs fields for tenant versus owner, women-managed assets, disability and mobility constraints, caste or tribal access, phone sharing, livestock ownership, evacuation transport, and the authority to open a gate or use a shelter.

### 4.5 Local agronomic validation

No global example should override Odisha Agricultural University, ICAR, IMD, DAFE, fisheries, livestock, or district disaster-management guidance. Odisha's official 2025 contingency plan already distinguishes drought, flood, cyclone, sowing windows, water management, and critical crop stages [34]. The missing step is machine-readable conversion, district validation, and field testing.

### 4.6 Evidence governance

Every rule needs source, date, geography, hazard class, crop and stage, confidence, expiry date, approving authority, and contraindications. Without these fields, a real practice can become dangerous filler when moved to a different ecology.

**Decision insight:** The next research budget should not collect more picturesque examples. It should quantify thresholds, costs, failure modes, and present-day users for the Grade A and B practices.

## 5. HOW IT FEEDS THE ADVISORY ENGINE: WHICH PRACTICE BECOMES WHICH RULE

### 5.1 Required decision architecture

Use the WMO people-centered multi-hazard early-warning framework as the backbone: **risk knowledge -> detection and forecast -> communication -> preparedness and response** [2]. Add an anticipatory-action layer that releases a specific task before impact, and a recovery layer that records damage, verifies safety, and recommends the next agronomic step.

The minimum farm state should contain geolocation; elevation; drainage outlet; embankment side; forecast water depth, rise rate, duration, velocity, and salinity; wind exposure; crop, variety, age, and maturity; seed quantity and storage height; livestock species and headcount; fodder and water; machinery; phone/IVR language; shelter; and mobility constraints.

| Livestock evacuation | Forecast inundation plus species/headcount and route | Sequence identification, movement, fodder, water, shelter, and veterinary follow-up | Separate animal message and volunteer task list | Human evacuation takes priority; avoid moving animals through deep/fast water [22] |
| Post-flood paddy recovery | Water receded and access declared safe | Ask salinity, silt, crop survival, stage, pest symptoms, and seed availability before advice | Branching IVR, photo optional, agronomist escalation | Never issue blanket fertilizer, pesticide, or reseeding advice without diagnosis |

### 5.2 Low-literacy interaction pattern

Use short, single-action voice messages in Odia and relevant tribal languages, with replay, keypad confirmation, missed-call recovery, and live-agent escalation. The existing Odisha service already supports toll-free short-code access, IVR, live agents, personalized profiles, and historical advisories [30]. Avaaj Otalo also demonstrates that voice forums can serve low-literacy, computer-novice farmers through recorded questions and answers [35].

Do not compress a village plan into a long SMS. A farmer should hear: **what happened, what to do now, where to go, who can help, and what not to do**. The system should log delivered, heard, acknowledged, completed, failed, and help-requested states.

**Decision insight:** The global wisdom layer should be a contraindication-aware rule registry, not a chatbot retrieval pile. Every recommendation must know when it is unsafe.

## Synthesis

| Mechanism family | Representative cases | Operating scale and horizon | Main benefit | Main trade-off or failure mode | Odisha decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Biological and calendar fit | Mekong floating rice, Inner Niger rice, Sub1, recession cropping | Seed to season; field/farm | Keeps production compatible with expected water | Fails when depth, duration, velocity, salinity, or timing exceeds tolerance | **Deploy first**, but only with variety and hazard constraints |
| Vertical separation | Baira, machan, Thai orchard beds, waru waru, chinampas | Household to field; before season or permanent | Keeps roots, seedlings, seed, feed, and small assets above shallow water | Overtopping, erosion, anchoring failure, labor, contaminated or saline water | **Pilot selectively** in calm waterlogged zones |
| Distributed landscape storage and drainage | Ifugao/Hani terraces, Huzhou ponds, Sri Lankan tanks | Watershed; years to generations | Slows, stores, routes, and reuses water | Maintenance burden, land requirement, gate conflict, catastrophic failure if badly designed | **Plan through panchayat/watershed programs**, never as instant advice |
| Exclusion and controlled space | Mekong dikes, US levees, Dutch polders/Room for the River | District to nation; decades | Protects concentrated assets or allocates safe flood space | High cost, residual risk, transferred flooding, false security | **Use the zoning principle**, not copied structures |
| Wind and homestead buffering | Ryukyu windbreaks, mixed home gardens | Homestead; multi-year | Reduces wind exposure and diversifies food | Tree fall, shade, salt injury, fragmentation gaps | **Pilot designed shelterbelts**, not ad hoc dense planting |
| Cooperative governance | CPP, Subak, tank villages, Dutch Delta Programme | Community to nation; continuous | Turns forecasts and shared infrastructure into coordinated action | Depends on trust, maintenance, inclusion, authority, and rehearsal | **Make named institutions part of every rule** |
| Recovery and risk transfer | Philippine replanting, USDA workflows, Ama Krushi | Farm to program; days to years | Speeds assessment, replacement, support, and learning | Delayed aid, exclusion, unsuitable replacement material | **Digitize damage evidence and route to human experts** |

Three tensions matter. First, flood exclusion and flood accommodation are not opposites to choose universally: protect people and irreplaceable assets, but allocate safe water space where repeated exclusion is uneconomic. Second, heritage systems are durable because institutions maintain them; extracting only the visible structure removes the mechanism that made it work. Third, biological tolerance is cheap and scalable but narrow, while civil works cover more hazards but create cost, governance, and failure liabilities.

The synthesis therefore favors a portfolio: Grade A household and warning actions now, Grade B agronomic and field pilots after validation, Grade C landscape investments through public planning, and Grade D quarantine for unsupported claims.

## 7. VERDICT

Build the global wisdom layer, but do **not** build it as a gallery of exotic traditions. Build it as a versioned rule base with six mandatory fields: **hazard fit, farm fit, action, lead time, contraindication, and accountable authority**.

The immediate Odisha release should contain: authenticated IMD-to-village warning chains; sealed and elevated seed/feed storage; crop-stage and variety-aware paddy rules; livestock evacuation and fodder workflows; drainage and bund inspection; raised nurseries and homestead assets; post-flood salinity and crop-survival diagnosis; geotagged damage assessment; and IVR acknowledgment with live-agent escalation. Bangladesh CPP and baira, flood-tolerant rice, Philippine recovery, USDA documentation, and Odisha's existing voice infrastructure provide the strongest starting points [1][3][23][30].

The next tier should test rice-fish or lotus systems, raised beds, shelterbelts, retention ponds, and watershed vegetation in controlled pilots. Do not automate large dikes, imported fish, unverified vernacular practices, pesticide recommendations, or sowing after recession until local water, soil, contamination, tenure, and weather conditions are checked.

Ama Krushi evidence reports tailored advisories at multi-million-farmer scale and reductions in severe crop-loss probability in evaluated settings, including a **9% harvest increase in excess-rainfall areas** [30]. That does not prove the proposed global rule set will work. It does show that Odisha already has a credible delivery and experimentation platform.

**Final decision:** Proceed with a minimum safe wisdom layer made only from Grade A actions and a small number of locally validated Grade B rules. Keep Grades C and D visible to planners and researchers, but block them from automatic farmer advice. The defining success metric is not messages sent; it is verified protective action completed without harmful transfer from the wrong ecology.
