# KrishiSetu Integration Map for Odisha Disaster Agriculture

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

- **Build on the warning chain, not beside it**: OSDMA is a post-1999 autonomous state institution under the Revenue and Disaster Management Department, while its current governing body is chaired by the Chief Secretary and includes Agriculture, Water Resources, Home, Panchayati Raj, the Special Relief Commissioner (SRC), and the OSDMA Managing Director [18][27]. KrishiSetu should therefore treat IMD/OSDMA/SRC as the hazard authority and Agriculture and Farmers' Empowerment as the advisory authority.
- **The last-mile backbone is real and technically useful**: Odisha's EWDS covers **1,205 villages in 22 blocks across six coastal districts**, with 122 alert towers, state/district/block activation, satellite and digital-mobile-radio links, mass messaging, and siren/voice/message delivery [3]. KrishiSetu's missing layer is not another cyclone siren; it is farm-specific advice attached to the official alert.
- **There is a prototype-ready hazard feed, but no proven OSDMA open-data contract**: IMD exposes a unified API gateway and CAP alert feed, and already disseminates to farmers through the Kisan Portal, to fishermen through INCOIS/NavIC, and to agencies through SMS and WhatsApp [36][37][29]. Use CAP/API/RSS for the prototype, but treat credentials, service levels, redistribution rights, and OSDMA data access as pilot gates.
- **Human escalation is explicit**: ODRAF has 20 roughly 50-person units, and normal deployment flows from the Collector and District Magistrate to SRC/OSDMA and then the police movement authority [19]. During Fani, trained Aapada Mitras reported to Block Development Officers (BDOs), spread warnings, evacuated vulnerable people, and supported shelters [22]. Product escalation must terminate in this chain, not invent a parallel emergency command path.
- **Dana demonstrates both state capacity and the personalization gap**: IMD first flagged likely cyclogenesis about **7.5 days before landfall**, issued 21 four-stage bulletins, and posted three-hourly social updates; Odisha later reported **800,000 evacuees in 6,210 relief centres**, **3.595M people affected**, and about 5,800 damaged homes [30]. These are strong population-level outcomes, but none is a per-plot, crop-stage action engine.
- **Correct the crop-assessment claim before pitching**: the official 2015 modality uses a **33% crop-loss threshold**, a two-day eye-estimation survey where appropriate, a three-day objection window, a separate seven-day crop-cutting process, and e-transfer of input subsidy [12]. The searched primary sources do **not** substantiate a Dana-specific "seven-day assessment of 5,428 acres"; that exact number should not appear in a demo or deck.
- **Shelter numbers require denominators and dates**: the 23 October 2024 Dana briefing had **842 permanent multipurpose cyclone/flood shelters ready plus 5,402 temporary buildings**, while a later secondary report says the statewide network is 909 shelters [7][31]. Dana also exposed shelters that failed expectations, prompting an overhaul [16]. KrishiSetu may route to an authority-approved shelter list, but should not hard-code a statewide count or inferred capacity.
- **The strongest farm-data bridge is Krushak Odisha, not a presumed KALIA payout API**: the government portal records Aadhaar-linked registration, mobile/contact details, crop details, and parcel identifiers such as Khata and Plot numbers, but its public FAQ discloses no API, consent protocol, or third-party access mechanism [25]. Use synthetic or explicitly consented profiles in the prototype; request formal access for a pilot.
- **Decision**: **GO** for a bounded prototype using official IMD alerts, synthetic/consented farm profiles, a KVK-reviewed rule library, and SMS/IVR. **PARTIAL** for a district pilot because institutional sponsorship and field validation are feasible but not pre-authorized. **GATED** for production integration until data-sharing, telecom, agronomic sign-off, consent, security, shelter-master, and escalation agreements exist.

## 4. WHAT IS MISSING

1. **The Dana "5,428 acres in seven days" source is missing.** Exact-phrase and official-domain searches did not recover it. The official modality instead distinguishes a two-day eye-estimation survey from a seven-day crop-cutting process [12], and the Dana briefing gives no deadline [7]. This is the highest-priority factual correction.
2. **A current authoritative shelter master is missing.** Required fields are shelter ID, coordinates, type, owning department, CSMMC contact, human capacity, accessible spaces, current power/water/toilet status, open/closed state, route, and last inspection. The 842 ready count on 23 October 2024 and the later 909 network count are not interchangeable [7][31].
3. **A dedicated shelter-plus-livestock program specification is missing.** Official material supports animal rescue [7], not a published livestock capacity or location inventory. Fodder, veterinary, tethering, species limits, and farmer-animal co-location are unresolved.
4. **Yaas after-action evidence is incomplete.** A primary Odisha report covering warning reach, district evacuation, force split, farm damage, shelter performance, and lessons was not found. Multi-state NDRF numbers are unsafe substitutes.
5. **Current SATARK status is unclear.** The source documents an experimental 2019 app [21]; no current official store listing, API, export, uptime statement, owner contact, or data license was verified.
6. **No OSDMA/open-data interface was found.** The website is an institutional source, not an operational data contract. IMD CAP/API is the better prototype entry, but credentials, rate limits, and redistribution terms still need confirmation.
7. **Krushak Odisha access governance is missing publicly.** The portal has exactly the useful crop and parcel fields, but no third-party API, consent text, purpose limitation, retention policy, webhook, or bulk-export process appears in the FAQ [25].
8. **KALIA is not verified as the cyclone-compensation rail.** Do not promise automatic KALIA payments. The sourced process is Agriculture/Revenue assessment followed by input subsidy and e-transfer [12].
9. **Advice content is missing as a governed artifact.** KrishiSetu needs crop x stage x hazard x lead-time x soil/drainage action cards in Odia, with KVK author, review date, contraindications, and confidence. Existing official alerts do not expose this joined schema.
10. **No formal startup lane is public.** Missing items include sponsor, MoU template, data-processing agreement, security review, procurement path, pilot budget, message-sender approval, IVR short code, farmer-consent script, grievance owner, and emergency liability boundary.
11. **Outcome evaluation is missing.** Existing evidence supports mass evacuation and institutional maturity, but not the causal effect of personalized advice on crop loss, message comprehension, false alarms, or excluded users. A Phailin study uses household surveys but evaluates cyclone exposure and a livelihoods intervention rather than EWDS itself [34].

### Case study: Fani shows why edge and offline matter

Fani combined unusually strong mobilization with infrastructure failure. The IRCS assessment records **1,470,197 evacuees**, 20 ODRAF units, 50 NDRF teams, village-level volunteer alerts, and **152,985.40 ha** of agriculture affected [28]. It also reports broad telecom outage and complete telephone/cellular failure in Puri [28].

The product mechanism is therefore store-and-forward, not cloud-only personalization. Before landfall, a block edge node should cache the latest official alert, local farm profiles, approved action cards, shelter/contact data, and IVR scripts. After connectivity loss, it should continue local SMS/voice queues and accept offline damage evidence, then synchronize without duplicating alerts when service returns. The implication is practical: a polished online dashboard without outage behavior is filler.

### Case study: Dana separates warning success from shelter and farm readiness

Dana had a first development signal about 7.5 days before landfall and 21 four-stage bulletins [30]. The state prepared 842 permanent and 5,402 temporary shelters, deployed 51 ODRAF, 178 Fire Service and 20 NDRF teams, and planned evacuation of about 1.06M people from 3,026 vulnerable villages [7]. Yet 3.595M people were affected and shelter performance later triggered an overhaul [30][16].

The mechanism is a capacity gap between knowing that a cyclone is coming and knowing what a particular farmer should do before rain, wind, surge, or flood reaches a particular crop stage. KrishiSetu should use the long lead window as a sequence: verify profile -> deliver crop-stage action -> collect acknowledgement -> escalate official evacuation -> switch to damage-safety and evidence capture. It should not advertise more accurate cyclone prediction than IMD.

### Practical pilot route

1. **Sponsor pair**: Agriculture and Farmers' Empowerment owns agronomy and farmer workflow; OSDMA/SRC owns disaster coordination and authoritative escalation. Secure a joint concept note rather than approaching EWDS as an independent broadcaster.
2. **District owner**: obtain Collector/DDMA approval because district mobilization and local response sit in that chain [19]. A sensible evidence-backed candidate is Kendrapara or Bhadrak: both were among Dana's worst-hit districts, and Kendrapara is named in the DAMU/KVK network [30][33].
3. **Block operators**: BDO/BEOC, VAW/AO, RI/Tahasil, KVK scientist, CSMMC and Aapada Mitra. This mirrors the documented warning, evacuation, and crop-survey chain [22][12].
4. **Pilot scope**: two blocks, two or three major crops, 500-1,000 consenting farmers, one pre-monsoon simulation and one live low-severity event. These are proposed design parameters, not sourced government commitments.
5. **Success metrics**: delivery and IVR completion, comprehension, action adoption, false/actionable alert ratio, worker time saved, time to create survey-ready evidence, inclusion by gender/literacy/phone type, and observed avoidable loss. Do not use evacuation count as the farm-product KPI.

| Decision level | Verdict | Why | Required next gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hackathon prototype** | **GO** | Authoritative IMD hazard feeds exist; OSDMA's chain and channels are known; Krushak supplies a realistic farm-profile schema; official assessment rules can power post-event checklists; SMS/IVR and edge caching directly answer the problem statement [36][3][25][12]. | Clearly label synthetic data and simulated government interfaces; use only KVK-reviewed advice; preserve official alert text and source timestamp. |
| **District field pilot** | **PARTIAL** | The organizational route is credible: Agriculture + OSDMA/SRC sponsorship, Collector/DDMA approval, BDO/BEOC operations, KVK agronomy, VAW/RI workflow, CSMMC/Aapada assistance [19][22][12]. But no public self-service pilot route or data authorization exists. | MoU, district order, farmer consent, data-processing terms, KVK content sign-off, approved telecom sender/IVR, shelter/contact master, field drill, grievance process, evaluation protocol. |
| **Statewide production / official branding** | **GATED** | The research did not verify OSDMA write access, a live shelter API, current SATARK operation, Krushak API/consent, KALIA payment linkage, a livestock-shelter registry, final Yaas evidence, or the 5,428-acre claim. | Security and privacy review, procurement, SLAs, official data-sharing, model/rule governance, 24x7 ownership, message authority, accessibility audit, independent impact evaluation. |

**Overall verdict: PARTIAL.** KrishiSetu is a strong and statement-faithful prototype because it fills a real join that the sourced ecosystem does not expose: **official hazard x farm parcel x crop stage x lead time -> one safe action over SMS/IVR**. It is not yet an officially integrated public service, and it should not be presented as one.

## Synthesis

Odisha's ecosystem uses three distinct mechanisms. **IMD** supplies meteorological truth and multi-channel warning; **OSDMA/SRC/DDMA/BDO and response forces** turn that warning into evacuation, shelter and rescue; **Agriculture/Revenue/KVK and the farmer registry** turn observed damage and crop context into assessment, advice and input subsidy. Their scopes, evidence bases and time horizons differ: IMD works from days to hours before impact, the response chain works from hours through immediate relief, and agricultural assessment works from days into recovery.

Fani and Dana expose the key tension. Fani shows that mass mobilization can coexist with total local telecom failure [28]. Dana shows that long warning lead and large-scale evacuation can coexist with millions affected and shelters that later require management reform [30][16]. The non-obvious implication is that "more warning" is not the main product opportunity. The opportunity is **conversion reliability**: convert an official alert into a farm-stage action before connectivity fails, then convert a farmer's post-event observations into a survey-ready, non-authoritative evidence packet.

The state system optimizes population safety, command legitimacy and relief administration. KrishiSetu should optimize comprehension, action timing, plot relevance and continuity while remaining subordinate to that command system. Its moat is not an unsourced "AI forecast"; it is a governed ruleset, consented parcel context, edge operation, low-literacy delivery, auditable provenance, and fit with the Collector-BDO-VAW/KVK chain.
