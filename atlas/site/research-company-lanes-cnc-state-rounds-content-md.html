Case study: why the RVCE search result is a failure case

A candidate should also contain at least one of the fields being sought, such as problem, challenge, judging, winner, pitch, or repository. The evidence layer contains three real but limited findings. First, event-shaped Unstop pages for Odisha, Maharashtra, and Telangana were discoverable, but the retrieved pages exposed cookie-disabled shells rather than content [VERIFIED] [1] [4] [3]. Second, social and organizer surfaces were indexed but did not provide usable descriptions [VERIFIED] [2] [5] [6] [2]. Third, searches for RVCE, D3, and rewind repositories were dominated by unrelated results, including institutional pages, sports pages, and generic GitHub projects [VERIFIED] [7] [9] [16] [12] [17]. These facts support a gap report, not a historical narrative.

The preparation layer is necessarily different. A configurable workflow shell has broad scope and low domain commitment; its trade-off is that it may look generic until the team adds a prompt-specific differentiator. A grounded ingestion layer has high leverage for education and document tasks; its trade-off is dependency and evaluation risk. An audit and resilience layer has less visual novelty; its mechanism is trust, and its trade-off is that it consumes time without creating a flashy feature. Across the five inferred shapes, the best strategy is to keep these layers separate so one domain adapter can be swapped in after the prompt arrives.
